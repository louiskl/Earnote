The note

Not a wall of transcript.

Earnote writes what you would have written yourself if you had been fast enough: a short summary, the topics that actually came up, decisions, and tasks you can tick off. Edit any of it, or summarise again with your own instruction — the AI version is one click away.

Listen back

Every timestamp is a button.

When the lecturer went too fast, click the time in the transcript and hear exactly that moment. The paragraph that is playing stays highlighted while you read along.

Want both at once? ⌘3 puts the note and the transcript side by side, and the transcript scrolls along with the audio.

Privacy

Nothing leaves your Mac.

Transcription runs locally with Whisper. The notes are written by a language model that Earnote downloads once and then runs on your device — no account, no API key, works offline.

Your audio stays put

Recordings, transcripts and notes live in your home folder and are never synced.

No telemetry

The app sends no analytics and no crash reports. There is no server that belongs to Earnote.

Cloud only if you ask

You can pick Claude, OpenAI, Gemini or your own server — the app says clearly when text would leave.

Questions

Good to know.

Is it really free?
Yes. Free, open source under the MIT licence, no accounts, no paid tier. A student project, not a business.
How fast is it?
On an M1 MacBook Air a 60-minute lecture is transcribed in about five to six minutes (measured: roughly 12× real time), and the note takes a few minutes more — you watch it being written. Since Earnote transcribes while you record, most of it is done by the time you stop. Low Power Mode makes everything about three times slower; Earnote tells you when that happens.
What do I need?
macOS 15 or newer on Apple Silicon and about 8 GB of memory. On the first run it downloads the speech model (~1.6 GB) and the local AI (~2.3 GB) — once, and it tells you what it is doing.
Does it work with Zoom, Teams and Meet?
Yes. Earnote captures system audio alongside your microphone, so everyone on the call ends up in the transcript.
Which languages?
The interface follows your Mac: English or German. Recordings can be transcribed in many languages, and you choose the language of the notes separately.
Am I allowed to record this?
Ask the people in the room first — in Germany § 201 StGB applies to private conversations. Earnote shows a reminder before each recording; the responsibility is yours.
Is there an iPhone version?
Yes, in testing right now. Put your phone on the desk, record, screen off. The note is written by the iPhone itself (iPhone 15 Pro or newer), by your Mac, or by a free AI with your own key. It should be in the App Store for the winter semester, iPad follows. There is no Windows version, because Earnote builds on Apple’s CoreML and MLX.
Why isn’t it in the App Store?
The sandbox there would get in the way of recording system audio and filing notes into other apps. The download here is signed and notarised by Apple, so it opens with a double-click.
